# Archived computational methods

Bioinformatic screening and comparative sequence analyses
SM1. Sequencing records, alignment and depth calculation
Genomic DNA for resequencing was extracted from muscle tissue, as described in Section 2.2 of the main text. Paired-end libraries were prepared by Shanghai Majorbio Technology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). The archived project report identifies the sequencing platform as Illumina NovaSeq X Plus and describes PE150 sequencing with an approximately 350-bp library-fragment target. Its raw bases/count ratio is 302 for all samples, consistent with 2 151-base reads per counted pair; actual FASTQ lengths and any trimming require confirmation. The current-reference workflow records fastp v0.23.2 filtering, followed by alignment to ASM4018292v1 (GenBank GCA_040182925.1; Zhao et al., 2024) using BWA-MEM v0.7.19 (Li & Durbin, 2009), addition of read groups, coordinate sorting and indexing, duplicate marking with SAMtools v1.23.1 (Danecek et al., 2021), and an archived coverage branch using mosdepth v0.3.13 (Pedersen & Quinlan, 2018). ASM4018292v1 was selected to use the latest publicly available chromosome-level reference genome at the time of reference selection. The existing mapping_qc_full.tsv file contains 1 Mean_depth value for each of the 24 samples; these values are reported in Table 1 at 2 decimal places. The accompanying server audit identifies the archived coverage inputs as coordinate-sorted BAMs preceding duplicate marking, with a full-reference denominator of 1,052,771,736 bp. The mean of the recorded sample depths is 19.73×, with a range of 14.80–39.25×. These values describe coverage before duplicate marking. Complete execution logs and original per-sample mosdepth summaries were unavailable for direct verification.
SM2. Variant sets and site-level filtering
Variants were identified using GATK v4.6.2.0 with HaplotypeCaller, GenomicsDBImport and GenotypeGVCFs (McKenna et al., 2010). The analysis used 2 variant sets. The population-genomic set retained PASS biallelic SNPs through SelectVariants; its VCF header records filters for QD < 2, FS > 60, SOR > 3, MQ < 40, MQRankSum < −12.5, ReadPosRankSum < −8 and QUAL < 30. The marker-screening sets were separately restricted to biallelic SNPs or InDels using BCFtools v1.23.1, excluding sites with QUAL < 30, total missingness > 0.20 or MAF < 0.05 (Danecek et al., 2021). Sample missingness and heterozygosity were summarized with scikit-allel v1.3.13. PCA, K-means clustering (k = 2) and Ward clustering were descriptive assessments of sample structure; they do not by themselves exclude kinship or population confounding. Complete executed commands, per-filter counts and genotype-level quality handling remain incompletely documented; no new genotype masking or kinship analysis is reported. For a targeted audit, SNP2 and Marker10 records were retrieved from the indexed archived SNP and InDel VCFs, respectively, with sample identities read directly from each VCF header. Original GT, DP, GQ and AD values are reported without altering the calls (Repository Tables R6A–R6B). This record check does not constitute new variant calling or a genome-wide association analysis.
SM3. Exploratory genomic screening and interpretation limits
Exploratory screening used 10-kb windows of the population-genomic SNP set. Signed ΔAF is AFfemale − AFmale; mean |ΔAF| summarizes the absolute difference across sites. The available FST code calculated the Hudson numerator and denominator at each SNP, truncated negative site estimates to zero and averaged site ratios within windows (Hudson et al., 1992). This differs from taking the ratio of summed numerators to summed denominators. In addition, the extreme ΔAF window had no matching FST window; the server audit identified a missing female genotype group treated as allele frequency zero. Such a group provides no valid allele-frequency estimate. These outputs cannot support quantitative regional differentiation. Sex-biased heterozygous sites were selected using male heterozygosity > 0.80 and female heterozygosity < 0.20, or the reverse. The chromosome-ranking script standardized the female/male heterozygosity ratio H, female/male coverage ratio C and chromosome mean FST D across chromosomes and calculated S1 = −z(H) − z(C) + z(D) and S2 = z(H) + z(C) + z(D), ranking by max(S1, S2). The associated 99th-percentile cutoffs were descriptive, not significance thresholds. Coverage normalization, effective-site criteria and the interval-selection rule are incompletely documented. No adjusted association P values or verified interval-stability estimates are available. These procedures explain candidate selection without establishing a sex-determining region.
SM4. Candidate selection and positional annotation
Gene models overlapping Chr19:10.5–14.2 Mb were retrieved from the GFF annotation associated with ASM4018292v1. Selected predicted proteins were compared with reviewed Swiss-Prot proteins using BLASTP v2.12.0 with E-value < 1 × 10−5 (Camacho et al., 2009). Functional names were treated as homology-based predictions and retained alongside stable locus tags and protein accessions. Gene prioritization in the original analysis was exploratory and considered local differentiation signals and biological plausibility; it was not a systematic test of gene function. The available 118-gene summary contains functional assignments for 5 genes, leaving 113 unannotated in that summary. General Chr19 candidate screening used |ΔAF| ≥ 0.35, total missingness ≤ 0.30, at least 7 called individuals per sex, and no more than 20 neighbouring variants within 100 bp. The candidate-gene-window workflow used |ΔAF| ≥ 0.45, total missingness ≤ 0.25, at least 7 called individuals per sex, and no more than 10 neighbouring variants within 100 bp; 3 SNPs were retained per targeted window. These downstream screens followed the initial variant-set filters. The 9 selected SNP coordinates were intersected with gene, transcript, exon and CDS features from the same GFF using 1-based inclusive coordinates. This establishes positional consequences only, not regulatory effects.
SM5. Public-sequence searches for the reported LG4 markers
The published male-specific primer pair and beta-actin control primers of Zhang et al. (2026) were searched against GCA_040182925.1, GCA_028476615.1 and GCA_009869775.1. Both strands of every reference sequence were scanned for full-length ungapped matches allowing up to 2 substitutions per primer. A set of 3 disjoint exact seeds followed by full-length mismatch counting ensured exhaustive retrieval within this substitution model. Candidate products required an inward-facing forward/reverse pair on the same sequence spanning 100–5000 bp, measured from the outer primer boundaries. NCBI BLAST+ v2.17.0 blastn-short searches provided a separate check of recovered full-length matches. Because the study also proposed sox10 as a candidate, public SOX10 proteins AGJ70265.1 and AGH25612.1 from common carp and NP_571950.1 from zebrafish were compared with the GCA_040182925.1 protein set using BLASTP (E-value threshold 1e-10; up to 30 targets). The zebrafish query was also searched against the genome using TBLASTN with the same E-value threshold, and the candidate locus was located using the reference GFF. The same full-length male-marker primer search was extended to the public male C. alburnus assembly associated with Ren et al. (2025), available from Figshare (doi:10.6084/m9.figshare.24125487.v1; FASTA file 42336459 and GFF3 file 42392196). The forward primer was GCAGTCACATGATCCTTTTTCTC and the reverse primer was GGTAACCAAATAGTTGGTGACC. Exact matches and matches allowing up to 2 substitutions per primer were evaluated separately. Product length was calculated from the outer primer boundaries. A predicted product with matching primer ends and reported length was treated as a marker-sequence anchor, not as proof that the public assembly was identical to the reference used by Zhang et al. (2026). No qualifying male-marker amplicon was found in the 3 initially examined assemblies. The SOX10-like match on Chr3 in ASM4018292v1 did not identify the specific LG4 copy described in the marker study. The public NGDC resource GWHDOEX00000000 is registered under BioProject PRJCA017093 and BioSample SAMC1263630. Its first sequence retains the original identifier tc_LG01, and the initial 214,066 bases match the corresponding Figshare sequence after case normalization. This limited repository cross-check does not identify the exact reference version or HiC_scaffold_4 sequence used by Zhang et al. (2026).
SM6. Alignment of the public marker anchor to ASM4018292v1
The predicted 1123-bp product from the Ren et al. (2025) assembly, its immediate 10-kb flanks and the combined 21,123-bp sequence were searched against all 37 sequences of ASM4018292v1 using BLAST+ v2.17.0 (Camacho et al., 2009). A sensitive blastn search used word size 11, no DUST or soft masking, and E-value ≤ 1e−10. A complementary megablast search used word size 28, DUST and soft masking, identity ≥ 90%, and E-value ≤ 1e−20. Both searches allowed up to 100 target sequences. To assess wider flanking support, the 100 kb on each side of the predicted product were divided into 40 nonoverlapping 5-kb tiles. These were searched using megablast with word size 28, DUST and soft masking, identity ≥ 95%, E-value ≤ 1e−50 and query coverage ≥ 70% per high-scoring segment pair (HSP). Query coverage was calculated from the query span of each HSP; dispersed hits across a chromosome were not combined to imply coverage of a single locus. Coordinates and detailed results are provided in Repository Table R5. These searches tested marker and flank correspondence, rather than whole-chromosome synteny or sex association.
